Our Manifesto

Architecture should make a place more legible.

We start with what is already there: light, trees, weather, and the materials worth keeping.

Komorebi makes buildings that belong to their place and grow better with time. Fewer materials. Better details. Room for life to change.

How We Work

A way of building, carried through every decision.

Our philosophy becomes useful when it changes the way a site is read, a detail is drawn, or a material is maintained.

01

Read the site first

We study weather, movement, existing vegetation, local craft, and the daily rituals already taking place before proposing a new form.

02

Use fewer, better materials

We choose materials for repairability, availability, and tactile character, allowing their changes over time to become part of the architecture.

03

Design the afterlife

Every assembly should anticipate maintenance, adaptation, reuse, and eventual disassembly rather than treating completion as an ending.

04

Leave room for change

A finished building should not feel frozen. It should accommodate different lives, seasons, and futures without losing its character.

Why Komorebi

A practice founded on what light reveals.

Komorebi is the Japanese word for sunlight filtering through leaves. It describes a changing condition, not a fixed object. That is how we think about architecture.

Our Beginning

Started with one small house and a question.

Komorebi was founded in 2018 by Ren Sato after a renovation project raised a simple question: what if a new building began by paying closer attention to what was already there?

That first project established the studio's way of working. Preserve what has value. Add only what is necessary. Let light, materials, and daily life guide the design.
